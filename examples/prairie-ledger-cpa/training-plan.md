## SESSION OUTLINES

### Session 1: AI fundamentals and usage rules (60 min)

| Time | Block |
|---|---|
| 0 to 10 | What Claude is and is not. It writes drafts and finds patterns. It does not know your clients, and it can be confidently wrong. Use the firm's own example: the wrong deduction answer. |
| 10 to 20 | The usage rules, one page, read together. Firm accounts only. What never goes in. A person always checks. |
| 20 to 30 | How to ask: situation, task, material, format. Live example using a cleaned firm document. |
| 30 to 50 | **Hands-on.** Each person does one real task from their own week (for example, tidy an internal procedure note, or summarize a cleaned engagement letter). The first answer is a draft. Ask for one change. |
| 50 to 57 | Checking the work. Each person finds one thing in their result they would change before using it. |
| 57 to 60 | Who to ask: the champions. |

**Afterward, each person can:** ask a clear request, improve a draft with a follow-up, and say what they must never enter.

### Session 2: Usage rules for admin (30 min)

| Time | Block |
|---|---|
| 0 to 10 | The usage rules, read together |
| 10 to 25 | **Hands-on:** one admin task of their choice (for example, a draft of an office notice) |
| 25 to 30 | Questions |

### Session 3: Karbon reminders and "Call client" list (30 min)

| Time | Block |
|---|---|
| 0 to 10 | What clients now receive, and when. Show a real reminder email. |
| 10 to 20 | **Hands-on:** each bookkeeper opens the "Call client" view and finds their own clients on it |
| 20 to 30 | What to do when a client replies, uploads late, or asks to stop reminders |

### Session 4: Client Answers (60 min)

| Time | Block |
|---|---|
| 0 to 10 | Why it only uses approved answers, and why the accountant always decides. Show a STOP FLAG and a NOT IN LIBRARY example. |
| 10 to 20 | Demo: one practice email, from paste to sent reply in Outlook |
| 20 to 45 | **Hands-on:** each accountant runs 3 of their own recent client emails (cleaned of SINs and bank details), edits the drafts, and notes the time it took |
| 45 to 55 | How to ask for a new approved answer. When the same question comes up twice, send it to the champion. |
| 55 to 60 | Time tracking for February |

### Session 5: Monthly Coding Check (45 min)

| Time | Block |
|---|---|
| 0 to 10 | What the check does and does not do. It flags. You decide. Show the 9 checks in plain words. |
| 10 to 15 | Demo on the practice café client |
| 15 to 35 | **Hands-on:** each bookkeeper exports one of their own clients' December transactions and payee history, runs the check, and reviews every flag |

### Session 6: Champions (60 min)

| Time | Block |
|---|---|

| 50 to 60 | Monthly check-in with Colette during the support period |

## LIKELY CONCERNS AND ANSWERS

| Concern | Who | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| "Will this replace me?" | An accountant | No. Dana has said so. These tools draft and flag. You still decide, edit and sign off. The goal is less admin and no Saturdays, not fewer people. |
| "It's a fad." | Marcy | That is fair to test. Judge it at 30 days on two numbers: hours spent chasing documents, and Saturdays worked. If it does not move them, we say so. |
| "I don't trust it. It got a deduction wrong." | Accountants | Good, and it should not be trusted alone. That is why Client Answers only uses answers your own accountants wrote, and why every reply is checked. In Session 4 you will see it refuse to answer what it does not know. |
| "I used ChatGPT with client info. Am I in trouble?" | 2 accountants | No. There were no rules before. Delete those chats, use firm accounts from now on. |
| "I don't have time to learn this." | Everyone | Each session uses work you already have to do this week. Nothing new starts in tax season. |
| "I'll look slow in front of others." | Less confident staff | Role sessions are small, and you work on your own screen. Champions are there for one-on-one help afterward. |

## USAGE RULES (STARTING DRAFT)

**Prairie Ledger CPA: Using AI at Work**
*Draft for Dana and Greg to review against the firm's professional obligations and privacy rules.*

**1. Use firm accounts only.**
Use only the firm's Claude workspace for work. Do not use personal ChatGPT, Claude or other AI accounts for anything to do with clients or the firm.

**2. Never enter these:**
- Social Insurance Numbers
- Bank account, credit card or login details
- Passwords
- Anything a client has asked us to keep confidential beyond normal practice

**3. Remove what is not needed.**
Before pasting a client email or document, remove details the task does not need.

**4. A person always checks.**
Nothing AI writes goes to a client until a staff member has read it, corrected it and sent it themselves. AI never recodes a transaction. A bookkeeper does.

**5. AI does not give tax advice.**
Client replies use only the firm's approved answers. Tax positions, CRA letters, missed deadlines and legal questions are always handled by an accountant or partner.

**6. When unsure, ask.**
Ask a champion (Priya or [champion accountant]), or Dana.

**7. Mistakes are reported, not hidden.**
If something goes wrong, tell a champion or Dana. We fix the rules, not blame the person.
